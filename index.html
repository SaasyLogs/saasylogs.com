It talks back

The log already knows what happened. It just won't say it in a sentence. Every tool I've used, including the two versions of this one I wrote before, hands you a tidier list of events — and a list has no row for "and then eleven seconds passed."

What happened

10.40 s — 60% of the whole transaction — is unlogged time, between SOQL_EXECUTE_END and DML_BEGIN.

Overall, 96% of the elapsed time has no log line explaining it.

2 updates cost 5.13 s across 5 rows; the slowest single one took 4.63 s. That span includes every trigger, flow and validation the save set off — not just the write.

Where the project actually is

Now is what I've run against a real log in a real spreadsheet. Next is what I'm building. Later is a wish list, and wish lists are free.

Now
Working and verified
  • A summary in sentences. Rubber duck debugging, except the duck goes first.
  • Unlogged gaps, ranked. Time your log doesn't account for. Nothing shows you this, because technically nothing happened.
  • The shortlist. Fatal errors, blown governor limits, and how close each namespace got.
  • The execution path. Every query, DML and method in the order it ran, nested so you can see what called what, with its own timing next to it. Filter it by trigger, class, method or object without re-running anything.
  • The automation you didn't know was running. Workflow rules, flows, validation rules and triggers, told apart and named. Most people debugging a flow are reading the wrong file.
  • Flow faults. When a flow takes its fault path, that's one line in a hundred thousand. It leads the summary instead of hiding in them.
  • Self-time hotspots. Which method burned the time, not counting its children.
  • It admits when the log is incomplete. Salesforce throws away chunks of big logs, and a log can stop before its transaction does. You get told, rather than getting confident maths done on the leftovers.
  • A scrub button. Hands back a clean copy of the log itself, for the ticket or the vendor. You pick what it keeps.
  • It tells you what's in there before you share it. The analysis is not scrubbed — it's your log and you're the one reading it. So the summary counts the emails, addresses and personal fields it found, and you decide.
  • No Drive permission. At all. It can only see the sheet you opened it from. That was the whole design, and it cost me some features.
Next
Being built now
  • The Marketplace listing, so you can install it normally instead of emailing me.
  • Open a log file instead of pasting it. The paste box is the ceiling, not the parser — a browser textarea gives up long before Apps Script does. Reading the file directly removes that, and still asks for no Drive permission.
  • Query fingerprinting. Eighty-seven queries that differ only by a bind value are one bug, not eighty-seven — that part works. What's left is arriving from a 101 email and finding the answer already at the top.
  • Before and after. Two logs of the same transaction, so you can tell whether your fix did anything.
  • Async honesty. Future calls and queueable jobs run as separate transactions with their own logs. Right now the tool measures a fraction and doesn't mention it. That's not great.
  • Naming the package. When the missing time belongs to a managed package, say which one instead of shrugging.
Later
Wanted, not started
  • Benchmarks you can keep. Capture a baseline, change something, run it again, and find out whether you helped. Then keep those runs as a suite. Most orgs have no performance regression testing at all — usually because nobody left knows what normal looked like.
  • Watch for one thing. You got the 101 email at 9am, so say that up front and have the analysis lead with it. Or look at flows and ignore triggers. Or search the whole log, not just the USER_DEBUG lines.
  • Twenty logs at once, to find the pattern one log can't show you.
  • Putting a transaction back together after Salesforce shattered it across several logs.
  • Enormous logs, in chunks. File upload raises the ceiling; chunking is what removes it.
  • Something visual. No promises about when.

🔒 Privacy Policy

Apex debug logs are among the most sensitive artefacts a Salesforce org produces. They routinely contain record IDs, email addresses, field values and the shape of your internal automation.

Saasy Logs does not collect, store, transmit or receive your log data. Parsing happens entirely inside the Google Apps Script runtime attached to your own spreadsheet. The add-on makes no external network requests, contains no analytics or tracking, and requests no Google Drive permission at all — it can only see the spreadsheet you open it from.

We operate no server and hold no account for you, so no data of yours ever reaches us. Nothing is stored, anywhere, for any length of time — not on a server, not in a cache, not between runs. An earlier version held your pasted text in Google's per-document cache for six hours so that you could re-run without re-pasting; that feature has since been removed, and the cache went with it.
